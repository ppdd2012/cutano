The distinction that matters

"Works with no signal" and "doesn't upload" are different sentences

Most marketing treats them as one promise. They aren't — and the reason they're worth separating is that each one is evidence for the other.

From no-upload to offline

  • If an image never leaves the device, there is no network step that can fail
  • So a genuinely upload-free app works on a plane by definition, not by design effort
  • Nothing is deferred, queued or synced later — there is no later to wait for
  • The reverse direction is the one that actually earns trust

From offline to no-upload

  • A tool whose steps require a server cannot complete them with the radio off
  • So watching it finish in airplane mode is a test, not a claim
  • One run doesn't prove an app's whole architecture
  • What is stated here is the strong version: the AI models run directly on your iPhone, and there is no upload path for images anywhere in the app

This page is mostly the second argument. If you can't risk a photo crossing a network, "it also works offline" is the cheapest way to check whether anyone is telling you the truth about where your images go — and it's the property that makes the app usable in rooms where no cloud tool is permitted in the first place.

Real situations

Who this page is actually for

Not people who dislike slow uploads. People for whom the upload is the problem.

Air travel

Seatback work on a long flight: cull a shoot, cut out the keepers, export what you'll need the moment you land. Nothing is paused waiting for a ground network.

Secure or regulated facilities

Rooms where phones get no cellular or Wi-Fi, and where no cloud service is permitted regardless. The requirement isn't convenience — an approved tool must not have a network path.

Field work

Sites, roads, forests, venues. A product shot or a document capture with nothing usable to send it to, and a deadline that doesn't move for a spinner.

Unreliable connectivity

Anywhere the connection is intermittent rather than absent. With a server pipeline you get partial results; with local inference you get the same result in a different amount of time.

Third-party images you don't own the right to send

A client's unreleased product, a patient-adjacent document, a vendor's catalogue shot. Sometimes the rule is simply that this file does not cross a network, and the conversation ends there.

Anyone who just doesn't want it

No account to open, no terms to reread, no reason to trust a retention policy. The cleanest privacy position is the one where the copy never exists elsewhere.

The honest remainder

What genuinely still needs a connection

Two things, and neither of them is the editing. Listing them is part of the point: an app that admits this is an app whose offline claim you can believe.

Goes through Apple

  • Installing the app, and updating it, from the App Store
  • Apple's own purchase flow, if you take a subscription or lifetime access
  • Restoring a past purchase, which is the same Apple account operation
  • Anything the App Store itself does — receipts, Family Sharing, billing

Needs nothing from a server

  • Every segmentation pass and every model switch
  • Edge refinement, brushes, layers, text, shadow and reflection
  • Batch runs and full-resolution export (Pro) into your photo library
  • The no-upload architecture on the free tier and for subscribers alike

There is no Cutano account to sign in to, which is why the airplane-mode answer is so much simpler than it usually is. Payments are handled natively through StoreKit 2 rather than a service of ours, so the only account in the loop is the one Apple already runs on your phone.

Why offline is the consequence, not the feature

Bundling the models is what makes the radio irrelevant

Offline support reads like a nice extra in most photo apps. Here it's a side effect of a decision made earlier: the thing that does the work travels inside the app.

The model ships with the app

A multi-model Core ML engine — BiRefNet-class on-device segmentation in multiple quality tiers — runs directly on your iPhone. If the model is on the phone, the photo has nowhere to go.

Refinement is local too

Metal GPU acceleration handles mask refinement, shadows and reflections on the device's own GPU. Post-processing is the half that usually gets pushed to a server; it isn't here.

The export is a local replay

Full-resolution layer replay on Pro means exporting at up to 8192px is a rendering job on your hardware, matching the preview pixel-for-pixel — not a file handed back to you.

The trade is honest: models that size make the app bigger than a thin client to a server would be, and their ceiling is what your iPhone can compute. The gain is that no outage, no captive portal and no policy memo can interrupt the work.

Older hardware

Offline work on a slower iPhone still finishes

Running locally means the constraint is compute time rather than bandwidth — and that's a problem the app tiers for. iPhone with iOS 17 or later is the requirement.

Strong device

  • Adaptive device tiering matches the workload to the machine's power
  • Batch scheduling is tiered, so the queue is built for this device
  • Real-time Metal rendering for shadow and reflection while you adjust

Older device

  • Automatic timeout fallback: a pass that runs long doesn't leave you stuck
  • Per-item retry in batch, so one difficult photo doesn't stall an album
  • Still entirely local — a slower phone costs minutes, not connectivity
  • What you can't do is borrow someone else's GPU. That's the actual trade of offline work.

FAQ

The questions this raises

Can I actually use it on a plane?
Yes. Airplane mode, seat 23A, no Wi-Fi offered at 38,000 feet: segmentation, model switching, edge sliders, brushes, layers, text, shadow, batch and full-resolution export (Pro) all run on the device. The only things you can't do mid-flight are things Apple does for you — downloading the app or completing a purchase.
Do I need an account before it will let me work offline?
No. There's no sign-up in the app, which is also why there's no sign-in step to fail when you have no connection. Tools that "work offline" usually mean they cache a session you established online first; there is no session here to establish.
What happens to the features when there's no internet?
Nothing, and that's the whole design. The features don't degrade, grey out, or queue for later because none of them were waiting on a server. Connection state isn't an input to the pipeline.
Can you see the photos I edit?
The app has no upload path for images anywhere in it, so nothing you edit arrives on our side — we don't hold a copy and there's no queue that sends one when you reconnect. What we can't speak to is your own device: your library lives under your iOS settings and your backups, which are Apple's territory. That's the honest boundary of the claim.
Does offline editing mean worse results?
No. Quality is set by which matting model you use, not by your connection, and all of them run locally. What does affect the ceiling is your plan — advanced models, layer compositing and AI shadows unlock through subscription — and on a slower iPhone, how long a pass takes. None of those are network questions.
