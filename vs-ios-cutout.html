Long-pressing a photo in the iOS Photos app is genuinely impressive one-shot segmentation — and it's the right tool for a quick paste into a message. It was never built to be an editing workflow. Here's the honest line between the two.

Give credit where it's due

What the built-in subject lift actually does

Since iOS 16, tapping and holding a person, pet or object in Photos runs on-device segmentation and lets you copy, share or drag the subject out. The recognition quality is good, and nothing leaves your phone — the same architectural point we make about Cutano.

Instant, zero setup

No app to install, no format to choose. Long press, then paste — it's the fastest route from "photo" to "cut-out" that exists on an iPhone.

Runs locally

The subject mask is computed on your device. There's no upload step, which is why it works in a secure office or on a plane.

Good enough for casual reuse

Memes, Messages stickers, a quick drop into a note. For a one-off subject with a clean edge, nothing else on the phone is faster.

Where it stops

Four problems the one-shot lift can't get past

The mask is final on arrival

  • No way to tighten a halo of leftover background
  • No way to recover the flyaway hairs it discarded
  • No second opinion from a different model
  • Cutano: contraction + feather sliders, brush refine, re-matte with another model

There's nothing to composite onto

  • No canvas, no layer order, no background swap
  • No text, no second subject, no positioning
  • What you copy is all you get
  • Cutano: unified layer model, multi-subject placement, AI shadow and reflection

It doesn't scale past one photo

  • Every subject is a separate long press
  • Ten product shots means ten manual passes
  • Cutano: batch matting with tiered scheduling and retry on the failures only

The output isn't a design file

  • No layered PSD, so a designer starts from scratch
  • No user-chosen resolution or format
  • Cutano: transparent PNG or JPG on every tier; layered PSD and original resolution up to 8192px on Pro
FAQ

Questions this raises

Is the iOS subject lift less accurate than Cutano?
Not necessarily — on a clean subject with a plain background, Apple's segmentation is genuinely good, and accuracy isn't the argument. The argument is control: it hands you one mask with no way to refine it, no second model, and nothing to composite it into.
Does Apple send my photo to a server when I lift a subject?
No. The subject lift runs on-device, which is the same architectural position Cutano takes. Both keep the image on your phone — the difference between them is workflow depth, not privacy.
Why would I install an app instead of using what's already there?
The moment you need anything beyond "subject by itself" — a white background for a listing, text over the cut-out, a shadow so it doesn't look pasted, twenty photos done in one pass, or a PSD a designer can reopen — the built-in lift stops being a tool and becomes a starting point.
Can Cutano do the thing where I just long-press and copy?
Yes, and faster in bulk. Import, auto-cutout, then export or share. The long-press flow is optimised for one casual reuse; Cutano is optimised for producing an image you can ship.
Which should I use?
Use the Photos app when you want a subject out of a picture in two seconds to paste somewhere. Use a dedicated cutout app when the output has to meet a spec — marketplace, print, or a design file someone else has to open.
